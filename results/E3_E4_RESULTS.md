Findings:
1. Without references, M1 helps at d=128 (0.326 -> 0.241) but hurts at d=16 (0.312 -> 0.352): standardising one gas stream also removes the concentration signal when the stream's concentration mix differs from the source.
2. With 5 references per batch, NLMS on the head (4 B) gives 0.154 at d=128 and block OLS 0.138 (2,565 B). At d=16 block OLS gives 0.184 with 325 B.
3. RLS with forgetting 0.8 (grid edge) diverges at k=20 for some gases (toluene 0.95 at d=16, ammonia 0.66 at d=128), the known covariance wind-up; k=1 and k=5 are stable.
4. Selected values on grid edges: head lambda 1000 (d=16), M5 mu 0.01 (d=16) and 1.0 (d=128), M6 0.8, M7 gamma 1000. Not re-tuned.

Findings:
1. Board-to-board variation costs more than day-to-day drift: a master model on a slave board gives 0.83 (worst pair 0.47 at d=64), while within a board accuracy stays at 0.87-0.95 over up to 17 days.
2. Zero labels: M1 on the slave stream gives 0.895 (d=16, 132 B) and 0.915 (d=64, 516 B).
3. Direct standardisation on the compact features (d=16, 1,088 B) reaches 0.925 with 8 transfer pairs and 0.955 with 16, worst pair 0.87 and 0.92. On the full features (d=64) DS needs 16.6 kB and is not better (0.919 and 0.946), so the 8 kB Mega2560 is not a limiting factor here.
4. Per-channel DS (M9) and paired affine (M4) give 0.89-0.90 with 128-192 B at d=16, about the same as M1 with no labels.
5. Day-drift hyperparameters were reused from the transfer selection; block OLS (M7) with gamma 100 loses accuracy there.
